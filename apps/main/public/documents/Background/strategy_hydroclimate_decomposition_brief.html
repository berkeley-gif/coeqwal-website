Reference Brief · Reading the Results

Separating the effects of strategy and hydroclimate in COEQWAL results

§2 What this page is

Every COEQWAL scenario combines two components: a management strategy and a hydroclimate. The strategy is the set of water management choices people can make. The hydroclimate is the pattern of temperature, precipitation, and flow conditions that no one controls. When a modeled result differs from one scenario to another, either component, or both, can be behind the difference.

This page explains how that difference can be separated into a part associated with the hydroclimate and a part associated with operations. It walks through simplified illustrations first, then shows one example from actual modeled results.

This page explains a method for reading modeled results from the COEQWAL ensemble; the numeric values in its illustrations are invented for teaching and the single worked example reports one modeled case. Splitting a difference into parts does not indicate whether any part, or the difference itself, is acceptable. COEQWAL does not recommend any course of action.

§3 Two components, one baseline

All comparisons start from one scenario, the baseline: current operations under the historical hydroclimate. It is the reference point every other result is read against. Every other scenario in the COEQWAL library changes the strategy, the hydroclimate, or both, and a scenario's difference from the baseline splits into two parts. The split holds operations fixed first: it uses current operations under the scenario's hydroclimate as a second reference point, so that each strategy is compared with current operations under the same hydroclimate.

The hydroclimate effect is the difference between current operations under that hydroclimate and the baseline. It answers: what changes if operations stay the same but the hydroclimate is different? The operations effect is the difference between the scenario and current operations under that same hydroclimate. It answers: what changes if the hydroclimate is the same but operations are different? The two effects add exactly to the scenario's difference from the baseline: baseline value + hydroclimate effect + operations effect = scenario value.

Two consequences follow. If a scenario keeps current operations, its operations effect is zero; if it keeps the historical hydroclimate, its hydroclimate effect is zero. And because the operations effect is measured under the scenario's own hydroclimate, the same strategy can have a different operations effect under different hydroclimates. That variation is how the interplay between strategy and hydroclimate shows up in results, and it is why the briefs report the operations effect separately for each hydroclimate.

Three notes for careful readers. First, the split applies only to continuous outcome values, not to outcome level categories; when a result crosses a level boundary, the crossing is caused jointly by everything that moved the value, and is not attributed to operations alone or the hydroclimate alone. Second, the split could be built the other way round, measuring the strategy first under the historical hydroclimate; COEQWAL uses the route through current operations so that every strategy is compared with current operations under the same hydroclimate. Third, the split describes where a modeled difference comes from. It does not say whether the difference matters, which is a judgment the framework leaves to the reader.

§6 Where this appears in COEQWAL

Readers will encounter this idea in the summary briefs on the Data page, where outlined table cells mark offsetting effects in the two-part form described above, and in comparisons across the Explore section, where the same result can be read against different strategies or different hydroclimates. The decomposition is a way of reading those materials, not a separate dataset.
